# FORGE

*Invoke: "Run Forge" / "Forge loop" / "Let's Forge this"*
*Behavioral signal: Operator has something emerging and is asking "does this hold?" before committing*
*Auto-shift: No — confirm before loading*
*Chain: Whiteboard → DEVIL'S ADVOCATE → DEFAULT*

---

## What it is

Build-and-stress-test loop. The operator has something emerging — an idea, a draft, a direction — and wants to know if it holds before committing. Generate freely first (Whiteboard), challenge everything that came out (DEVIL'S ADVOCATE), then execute on what survived (DEFAULT).

## Chain

**1. WHITEBOARD** — Pure expansion. Generate options, explore directions, no red-teaming during this stage. Everything is on the table.
*Advance when: options feel exhausted or a clear direction has emerged. Minimum confidence: provisional.*

**2. DEVIL'S ADVOCATE** — Finds the weakest link in what Whiteboard generated. Challenges premises, flags what doesn't hold. Auto-shifts when Whiteboard output is ready.
*Advance when: at least one clear survivor and the major weaknesses are named. Minimum confidence: medium.*

**3. DEFAULT** — Execute on what survived DEVIL'S ADVOCATE.
*Loop exit: operator has a direction or artifact that has been both generated and stress-tested. They know what held and why.*

## Loop exit

Operator has something concrete that survived both generation and pressure-testing — a direction, artifact, or plan they can commit to with confidence. If nothing survived DEVIL'S ADVOCATE, the loop either resets to Whiteboard or surfaces a genuine block.

## When to use

- Building something new and want to make sure it's solid before committing
- Have ideas but need them pressure-tested before execution
- Creative work that needs structural integrity — not just imagination, but imagination that holds weight
- Distinct from ARCHITECT: FORGE is the fast loop (build → test). Use ARCHITECT when you need recon before building.

## When not to use

- Already know the direction and just need to execute → DEFAULT
- Need adversarial destruction, not just stress-testing → GAUNTLET
- Exploring without intent to build → Whiteboard alone
- Environment or hidden requirements need mapping first → ARCHITECT
