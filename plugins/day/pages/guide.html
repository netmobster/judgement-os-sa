Judgement OS · field guide2026.10.05
Judgement OS*

A second opinion for your Claude Code skills, only when they need one.

*Not an operating system. The name is a joke; the plugins aren't.

What you just installed

01 // Start here

Eight Claude Code plugins that work from your selfActual vault: your tasks, your gears, your messages and your profile. Once a month they check that your selfActual account answers.

  • The judges. Inside a skill, a rule makes the few calls that depend on you. When the rule can't tell, two judges read your profile and answer one small question. You only hear about it when both disagree with the rule.
  • Your day. A short boot, check-ins, habits you set up once, and a daily reading and a phrase in a language you're learning.
  • Making things. Pages in a house style, forms in the chat, decks and docs, from one front door: /make.
  • Thinking tools. Gears, loops and modes through one router: "scout this", "grumpy on this", "root-cause it".
  • Hard rules. Nothing you send leaves until you type send, and the guard asks before anything destructive.

The whole story, with the install lines, is on the Judgement OS page.

PNL.00 // Is it for you
Use it if
  • you use Claude Code most days, and your skills keep hitting choices that depend on you
  • you'd rather write a skill as an objective than map every branch
  • you want a short daily loop from your own tasks
  • you want finished pages, and messages that wait for your yes
  • you have a selfActual account and want Claude Code working from your vault
Don't use it if
  • you want an agent that acts on its own: the judges recommend, they never authorise
  • you want a framework or an actual operating system
  • you don't use Claude Code: chat and the free plan can't run plugins
  • you need it battle-tested: it's young, in daily use since October 2026
  • you don't have a selfActual account: the General Edition is the one for you
PNL.01 // Before you start
First
Install the plugins, then open a new chat. Plugins load when a chat starts.
Talk
Say what you want. Each plugin listens for its own work, and the commands below are shortcuts.
Account
This edition works from your selfActual vault, so connect the selfActual connector first. Without it, the vault steps say so, and boot reads your project's TASKS.md.
Order
Step 1 first. The judges read your profile, and several steps change once it's filled in.
When done
Say guide done to Claude Code (CC on the buttons). It reads your answers off this page.

The steps

02 // 17 to try
Step 1 // Write your profile

Type /labs:profile

You should see A starter profile, if you have none yet. It never overwrites one. Then five questions, one at a time: who you are, how you work, what you're on, how you like things made, and when a judge should push back. It asks what your pages should call you. It asks once more before it switches your judges on.

Step 2 // Boot

Type /day:boot, or say morning

You should see A brief of three lines at most: the date, then the one or two things that matter today. It reads your tasks from your vault, and the next steps your last session left. The last line names boot's pick of style (bare, project or general) and why. Reply, and it asks your energy; name another style there to change it. With your profile filled in, the judges weigh in when the morning reads two ways, and you see their second opinion only when both disagree with boot's pick.

Step 3 // Habits

Type /day:habits

You should see The first time, it sets up your rows with you: what you do each day, which rows are core, and a focus. After that, it shows what's still open today and logs scores you give in plain words, such as walk 2, water 3. From tomorrow, boot offers them until they're logged.

Step 4 // The daily reading and a phrase

Type /day:daily

You should see The first time, it asks which you want: a reading each day, a phrase in a language you're learning, or both. It sets each one up with you and shows day 1. From tomorrow, boot shows the reading first and the phrase last.

Step 5 // A page

Type /sexyhtml a page of this week's plan, or ask for a page in your own words

You should see A finished page in your look, as a private link. The default look is ECHO-JAY: gold and steel, with For you on the nameplate, or your name once you've given it in step 1. Games come out in Seren. selfActual work comes out in the selfActual look. When a request reads two ways, the judges weigh in on the style. A−/A+ and DARK/LIGHT both work.

Step 6 // A form in the chat

Type /make ask me in chat which plugin to try next

You should see A form in the chat, with the recommended option first, an Other box and a notes box last. It keeps Claude's own look on purpose. Continue sends your answers back as your next message. With no form tool in the session, it asks with a picker instead.

Step 7 // A gear

Say scout this: I want to start a newsletter next month, or type /sa:gear for the menu

You should see One line naming the gear, such as → scout. Then it runs the gear and finds what your plan is missing. Typed alone, the command lists the gears, loops and modes, one line each. Your gears come from your vault, with a bundled set for when it isn't there.

Step 8 // The send gate

Ask for a test message to yourself through a messaging connector you have, such as send me a Slack DM saying gate test. Don't type send yet.

You should see Claude says the send gate stopped it, and shows you the exact text and who it goes to. Nothing goes out until you type send as your whole message. That opens the gate for one send. With no messaging connector there is nothing to gate, so leave this one unpicked and say so in the note.

Step 9 // Humanify a draft

Type /comms:humanify, then paste a draft you wrote with AI's help

You should see One line naming the register, picked from where the draft is going, or a question if it can't tell. An audit table comes first, before any edit. Then the cleaned draft and a report: the register, the signals before and after, and how much changed. A line saying AI helped stays as it is.

Step 10 // Poll a Slack channel

Type /comms:poll

You should see The first time, it asks which workspace and channels to read, and shows the file it will write before it writes it. Then it says what's new since your last post, thread by thread, with a draft where a reply is owed. Nothing posts until you approve a reply's exact text and type send. With no Slack connector, it says so and stops.

Step 11 // Dice

Type /dice:roll 2d20kh1

You should see Two d20s with the higher one kept, which is advantage. The total is in bold, both dice show, and the dropped one is struck through. A script rolls every die, and Claude never makes a number up.

Step 12 // Judge the judges, blind

Type /labs:try today

You should see Two answers on today's style pick, as Judge 1 and Judge 2, in an order the code drew. You pick the better one before you know which model wrote it. Then it tells you. If the rule stays out, say ask anyway. For the full test, type /labs:guide.

Step 13 // The clock and the check-ins

Ask what time is it?, then type /day:checkin

You should see The time in your time zone, from the clock on every message. After 13:00, Claude offers the mid-day check-in once, in one plain line, after it answers you. After 16:00 it offers the end-of-day one. /day:checkin runs one now: anything you held at boot, the habits still open, then your overdue and due-today tasks, one question each.

Step 14 // Open a session

Type /sa:open

You should see A short orientation: the session's number, your first name and the local time. Any P0 that's overdue or due today comes first, then the rest of your P0 and P1 tasks. It only reads. Nothing is written to your vault.

Step 15 // Close a session

Type /sa:close, at the end of a chat where you made or changed something

You should see A git check first, which flags anything uncommitted. Then the session log goes to your vault. Then two plain questions: should what you committed to become tasks, and should your profile change? Nothing else is written without your yes. A chat that only read things gets no log, and it says so.

Step 16 // Vault messages

Type /comms:messages

You should see Your vault messages, newest first, one line each. Opening one shows it in full. To send one, Claude shows you the exact text and who it goes to, and asks. Then the send gate stops it until you type send. That makes two locks, on purpose.

Step 17 // A selfActual page

Type /sexyhtml:sa a one-page summary of what I shipped this week

You should see A page in the selfActual look: paper and ink grounds with a hard cut between them, Caprasimo headlines, and an evidence tag on every claim (shipped, measured, building or hypothesis).
